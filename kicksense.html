KickSense

Build a home-use device for infants aged 3 to 9 months with cerebral palsy that detects one therapeutic movement, a knee kick with a stable thigh, and rewards it right away. No wires on the infant, under 5 minutes for a parent to set up, under $150 in parts.

My role
Tech lead. I wrote the firmware, built the electronics and designed the enclosure.
Context
Design Thinking and Communication, Northwestern. Client: M Street Pediatric Therapy.
Category
Physical computing

Process

  • Sensor in a plush toy, not on the baby. An ankle strap gave clean readings, but caregivers were uneasy about wiring near the infant. After the move, no caregiver raised a safety concern.
  • A moving toy as the reward. In testing, infants barely reacted to sound and consistently watched moving objects, so a servo turns a toy when a kick is detected.
  • An incline. Flat on the floor, wriggling registered almost as readily as kicking. Propping the infant up separates the two motions, so the firmware calibrates for the incline.
  • Layered filtering. Each filter removes one kind of false kick: gravity compensation removes the constant 1 g reading, a sustain buffer rejects single-sample spikes, a sign-change check rejects back-and-forth vibration, and adaptive recalibration follows a drifting baseline.

Results

  • Kick detection confidence rose from about 25% to about 60% with the incline.
  • Infants responded consistently to the moving toy.
  • Delivered a final report in IEEE format and a presentation to the client.
